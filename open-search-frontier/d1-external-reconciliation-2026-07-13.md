# D1 external reconciliation — Choudhary–Barbosa, arXiv:2411.09773 (2026-07-13)

R. Choudhary, R. S. Barbosa, *"Exclusivity principle, Ramsey theory, and n-cycle PR boxes"*, arXiv:2411.09773 (Nov 2024). Read in full (ar5iv rendering) for this note. Their setup: k identical copies of the n-cycle PR box (the α\* point — all 2n edge-events at probability 1/2, odd number of anti-correlated contexts); joint exclusivity structure refined to an **edge-colored exclusivity multigraph** Γ^k (one color per copy); every joint event has probability 2^(−k), so a k-copy E-principle violation ⇔ a clique K_{2^k+1} in Γ^k. Their engine: monochromatic projections H_i(Γ^k) inherit odd-cycle structure from the factor (their Prop. 2 / Cor. 3: the shortest odd cycle of H_i equals that of G_i), which lets multicolor **Ramsey numbers for odd cycles** rule cliques out.

## What their theorems prove (verbatim-checked statements)

- **Thm 9:** two copies activate for n = 4, 5 (K₅ exists; for CHSH and KCBS PR boxes the 5/4 violation).
- **R(K₃,K₃)=6 argument:** 5/4 is the *maximum* two-copy violation for CHSH **and KCBS** PR boxes (no K₆ since the exclusivity graph of any n-cycle PR box is triangle-free, their Cor. 5).
- **Thm 10 + Cor. 11:** for n ≥ 6, two copies contain no K₅ (hence no K_l, l ≥ 6) — **no activation at k=2** (uses R(≤(C₅,C₃))=5, Erdős–Faudree–Rousseau–Schelp 1976).
- **Thm 12:** for n ≥ 6, three copies contain no K₉ — **no activation at k=3** (uses R(≤(C₅,C₅,C₅))=9).
- **Sec. VI.B:** a non-extendable K_{2^k} always exists in Γ^k (OR-product of k K₂'s), so ω(Γ^k) = 2^k exactly in the no-activation regime.
- **Thm 13:** for n ≥ 2^k + 2, k copies contain no K_{2^k+1} (Day–Johnson 2017: every k-edge-coloring of K_{2^k+1} has a monochromatic odd cycle). **Open window they leave: 6 ≤ n ≤ 2^k + 1 for k ≥ 4**; they conjecture every n-cycle PR box activates at some k.

**ERG-001 (heptagon catalysis) is not subsumed — and their machinery explains it.** Their theorems cover *identical* copies only. The catalysis theorem (C₇ ⊗ C₇ ⊗ C₅ contains K₉, violating the product bound 8) sits exactly in the escape hatch their Ramsey argument leaves: any 3-coloring of K₉ must contain a monochromatic odd cycle of length ≤ 5 (R(≤(C₅,C₅,C₅))=9), the two C₇-colored projections cannot host odd cycles shorter than 7 (their Cor. 3 applied to C₇), so the short odd cycle is forced into the **pentagon catalyst's color** — which is precisely the color that can carry a C₅. The catalyst works because it is the unique factor whose odd-girth admits the Ramsey-forced cycle. This reading (a) makes ERG-001 a sharp *instantiation of the unique loophole in their Thm-12 mechanism*, (b) explains why n=7 is the boundary (their Thm 6: odd-girth of the n-cycle PR-box exclusivity graph = n for odd n), and (c) should be stated in any ERG write-up with both citations.

**ERG-003 (open nonagon cell) — a delegation-ready analytic attack now exists.** The cell C₉ ⊗ C₉ ⊗ C₉ ⊗ C₅ (violation ⇔ K₁₇, current bracket 16 ≤ ω ≤ 19, best-found 16) is a *mixed* 4-color instance inside their open k=4 window. The precise question their toolkit poses: **does every 4-edge-coloring of K₁₇ with three colors of odd-girth ≥ 9 and one color of odd-girth ≥ 5 (with each color class realizable inside the respective prism/Möbius-ladder projections, their Thm 4) exist, or can odd-cycle Ramsey counting exclude K₁₇?** Day–Johnson gives a monochromatic odd cycle in any 4-coloring of K₁₇; it lands legally in a C₉ color (9 ≤ 17) or the C₅ color, so the plain argument does *not* close it — but quantitative refinements (counting how many disjoint monochromatic odd cycles are forced, or Bondy–Erdős-type length-restricted Ramsey bounds R(C₉,C₉,C₉,C₅)) could push the upper bound below 17 and settle ERG-003 negatively without the clique search. Before delegating any further CPU-hours to clique search, this analytic route should be attempted — it is the cheap half of a bracket that several hundred CPU-hours have not moved.
